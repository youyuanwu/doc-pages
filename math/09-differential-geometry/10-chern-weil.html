Chern–Weil Theory

Chern–Weil theory manufactures topological invariants out of curvature: from any invariant polynomial of the curvature 2-form it builds a closed differential form whose de Rham class is independent of the connection. This is the machine that turns the local, connection-dependent field strength into global, quantized characteristic numbers — the instanton number, the monopole charge, the Euler characteristic. It builds on curvature.md and de-rham.md; the resulting classes are catalogued in characteristic-classes.md.

Invariant polynomials

Let P(X) be a symmetric, Ad-invariant polynomial on the Lie algebra g — P(g−1Xg)=P(X) — for example P(X)=tr(Xk) or det(1+X). Evaluated on the curvature 2-form F (with wedge products of the form-part), P(F) is an ordinary differential form of degree 2k on the base. Invariance is what makes P(F) gauge-independent: since F↦g−1Fg under a gauge change (curvature.md), an Ad-invariant P sees no change.

The Chern–Weil theorem

Chern–Weil theorem. For an Ad-invariant polynomial P and the curvature F of any connection:

  1. P(F) is closed: dP(F)=0 (using the Bianchi identity).
  2. Its de Rham class [P(F)]∈HdR2k​(M) is independent of the connection — changing A shifts P(F) by an exact form.

So while F itself depends on the choice of connection (a physical field), the cohomology class [P(F)] depends only on the bundle. That class is a characteristic class; its integral over a cycle is a characteristic number, a topological invariant.

Sketch. Closedness: dP(F)=kP(d∇​F,F,…)=0 by Bianchi d∇​F=0 and invariance. Connection-independence: interpolate At​=A0​+ta between two connections; dtd​P(Ft​)=d(something) is exact — the transgression / Chern–Simons form is that "something".

Chern–Simons forms

The exact form witnessing connection-independence is the Chern–Simons form. For the second Chern class, tr(F∧F)=dωCS​,ωCS​=tr(A∧dA+32​A∧A∧A). Locally tr(F∧F) is exact (it is dωCS​), so its integral over a closed manifold is topological — nonzero only because of global twisting. The Chern–Simons form ωCS​ is itself the Lagrangian of Chern–Simons theory (a topological field theory) and appears in the descent equations for anomalies (index-theorem.md, anomalies/chiral-anomaly.md).

Why this is the key theorem

Chern–Weil is the bridge that makes "topological charge = curvature integral" rigorous:

  • it explains why the instanton number 8π21​∫tr(F∧F) is an integer independent of the field configuration within a topological sector (characteristic-classes.md);
  • it is why the θ-term θ∫tr(F∧F) is topological (a total derivative locally) yet physically consequential globally (strong-CP);
  • it underlies the Gauss–Bonnet theorem when applied to the tangent bundle's curvature (riemannian-bridge.md).
