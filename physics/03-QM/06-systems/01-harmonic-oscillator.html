The Harmonic Oscillator

The one-dimensional harmonic oscillator is the single most important solvable system in quantum mechanics. Every potential looks harmonic near a stable equilibrium (Taylor-expand to second order), so the oscillator governs molecular vibrations, phonons, and — most importantly for what follows — the normal modes of a quantum field. Its algebraic solution via ladder operators is the direct prototype for the creation/annihilation operators of QFT Fock space.

The Hamiltonian is

H^=2mp^​2​+21​mω2x^2,

with [x^,p^​]=iℏ (see wave mechanics).

1. Ladder Operators

Define the dimensionless annihilation and creation operators

a^=2ℏmω​​(x^+mωi​p^​),a^†=2ℏmω​​(x^−mωi​p^​).

From the canonical commutator these satisfy the defining algebra

[a^,a^†]=1.​

Inverting, x^=ℏ/2mω​(a^+a^†) and p^​=iℏmω/2​(a^†−a^), the Hamiltonian becomes

H^=ℏω(a^†a^+21​)=ℏω(N^+21​),N^≡a^†a^,

with N^ the number operator (Hermitian, ≥0).

2. The Spectrum from the Algebra

The commutators [N^,a^]=−a^ and [N^,a^†]=+a^† mean the ladder operators shift N^-eigenvalues by ∓1: if N^∣n⟩=n∣n⟩, then a^∣n⟩ has eigenvalue n−1 and a^†∣n⟩ eigenvalue n+1. Since N^≥0, the lowering must terminate at a ground state ∣0⟩ with a^∣0⟩=0, hence n=0. Applying a^† repeatedly generates the whole tower:

a^∣n⟩=n​∣n−1⟩,a^†∣n⟩=n+1​∣n+1⟩,∣n⟩=n!​(a^†)n​∣0⟩.

The energy spectrum is therefore evenly spaced:

En​=ℏω(n+21​),n=0,1,2,…​

Two features have no classical analog: the ground-state (zero-point) energy E0​=21​ℏω>0, forced by the uncertainty principle (the particle cannot sit still at the minimum), and the exact equal spacing ℏω, which is what makes the modes of a free field behave as independent quanta of energy ℏω.

3. Position-Space Wavefunctions

The ground state follows from the first-order equation a^∣0⟩=0, i.e. (x+mωℏ​∂x​)ψ0​=0, giving a Gaussian:

ψ0​(x)=(πℏmω​)1/4exp(−2ℏmωx2​).

Excited states are obtained by applying a^†, producing Hermite polynomials Hn​:

ψn​(x)=2nn!​1​(πℏmω​)1/4Hn​(ℏmω​​x)exp(−2ℏmωx2​).

The Gaussian ground state saturates ΔxΔp=ℏ/2 — it is a minimum-uncertainty state.

4. Coherent States

The eigenstates of the (non-Hermitian) annihilation operator,

a^∣α⟩=α∣α⟩,∣α⟩=e−∣α∣2/2n=0∑∞​n!​αn​∣n⟩,α∈C,

are the coherent states. They are minimum-uncertainty Gaussians whose centroid ⟨x^⟩,⟨p^​⟩ traces the classical oscillation without spreading, giving the closest quantum analog of a classical oscillator. The photon number follows a Poisson distribution ∣⟨n∣α⟩∣2=e−∣α∣2∣α∣2n/n! with ⟨N^⟩=∣α∣2. Squeezed states redistribute the uncertainty unequally between x^ and p^​ while still saturating the product — the basis of sub-shot-noise metrology.

5. Forward Link: Fields as Oscillators

A free quantum field is a continuum of decoupled harmonic oscillators, one per mode k. The ladder operators become the mode creation/annihilation operators, ∣0⟩ the field vacuum, and ∣n⟩ a state of n quanta (particles) in that mode. The zero-point energy 21​ℏω summed over modes is the vacuum energy; the equal spacing is why particles of a given mode are identical. Everything on this page reappears there almost verbatim.
