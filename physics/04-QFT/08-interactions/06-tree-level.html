Tree-Level Worked Examples

This page walks the full computational pipeline — Dyson series → Wick's theorem → Feynman rules → LSZ → cross section — for concrete processes at tree level (no loops), where every amplitude is finite and closed-form. It complements the standalone Compton scattering calculation with simpler scalar and QED examples, and introduces Mandelstam variables and crossing.

Conventions: ℏ=c=1, ημν​=diag(+1,−1,−1,−1).

Mandelstam variables

For a 2→2 process p1​+p2​→p3​+p4​, the Lorentz-invariant kinematics are packaged in the three Mandelstam variables:

s=(p1​+p2​)2,t=(p1​−p3​)2,u=(p1​−p4​)2,

satisfying s+t+u=∑i​mi2​. Here s is the center-of-mass energy squared (the invariant mass of the initial state), while t and u are momentum transfers. Amplitudes are naturally written as functions M(s,t,u), and the three channels correspond to the three ways an internal particle can be exchanged.

Example 1: ϕ4 scattering ϕϕ→ϕϕ

The simplest possible amplitude. From Wick's theorem, the single four-point vertex gives, at first order in λ,

iM=−iλ⟹∣M∣2​=λ2.

There is no internal line and no angular dependence — the scattering is isotropic. The differential cross section from the master formula (identical particles, mass m, center-of-mass) is

dΩdσ​=64π2sλ2​⋅∣pi​∣∣pf​∣​=64π2sλ2​,

and the total cross section, with a symmetry factor 21​ for identical final particles, is σ=λ2/(32πs). This is the "hydrogen atom" of QFT calculations: every ingredient appears once, at its simplest.

Example 2: Yukawa theory ψψ→ψψ

Couple a Dirac fermion to a real scalar mediator via Lint​=−gψˉ​ψϕ. At tree level, fermion–fermion scattering proceeds by scalar exchange in the t- and u-channels (the two ways to connect identical external fermions), giving

iM=(−ig)2[t−mϕ2​uˉ(p3​)u(p1​)uˉ(p4​)u(p2​)​−u−mϕ2​uˉ(p4​)u(p1​)uˉ(p3​)u(p2​)​],

with the relative minus sign enforced by Fermi statistics (antisymmetry under exchange of the identical final fermions — a direct consequence of the anticommutator quantization). In the non-relativistic limit this amplitude reproduces the Yukawa potential V(r)=−4πg2​re−mϕ​r​ — the original point of Yukawa's theory: a massive mediator gives a short-range force of range 1/mϕ​, while a massless mediator (mϕ​→0) gives the long-range Coulomb form.

Example 3: QED e+e−→μ+μ−

The cleanest QED cross section, and the backbone of e+e−-collider physics. At tree level a single s-channel photon is exchanged. Using the QED Feynman rules:

iM=[vˉ(p2​)(−ieγμ)u(p1​)]s−iημν​​[uˉ(p3​)(−ieγν)v(p4​)].

Spin-averaging turns ∣M∣2​ into a product of two gamma-matrix traces. In the high-energy limit (s≫me2​,mμ2​) they evaluate to

∣M∣2​=2e4s2t2+u2​=e4(1+cos2θ),

giving the celebrated angular distribution and total cross section

dΩdσ​=4sα2​(1+cos2θ),σ=3s4πα2​,​

with α=e2/4π. The 1/s falloff and the total σ=4πα2/3s are textbook benchmarks, and the ratio of hadronic to muonic cross sections R=σ(e+e−→hadrons)/σ(e+e−→μ+μ−) is a classic measurement of the number of quark colors — see collider observables.

Crossing symmetry

The three examples above are related by crossing: the same analytic function M(s,t,u) describes different physical processes depending on which invariants are positive. Moving a particle from the initial to the final state (with p→−p) exchanges channels:

ProcessPhysical channel
e+e−→μ+μ−s-channel
e−μ−→e−μ−t-channel (crossed)

Crossing is a consequence of the analyticity of amplitudes (the same property underlying the optical theorem) and means a single calculation yields several cross sections. The Compton amplitude and pair annihilation e+e−→γγ are similarly crossing-related.
